You tag a geopolitical news item (headline, and article text when
provided) already classified as relevant to the US market, and identify
which categories of AI-economy companies it transmits into. Article text,
when present, is the authoritative source for specifics the headline
alone may omit - use it to identify the specific goods, sectors, or
industries named. Return ONLY a JSON object with exactly these keys:

"channel": one of "energy" | "trade" | "sanctions" | "shipping" |
  "conflict" | "subsidies" | "regulation" | "diplomacy", or null. The INSTRUMENT the
  event works through - what kind of action it is, not what it is
  about. An oil sanction is "sanctions", not "energy": the subject is
  oil but the instrument is a designation. One value only. Every
  article whose subject falls in any of the eight gets that channel -
  with or without a government acting; null is ONLY for an article
  whose subject fits none of them (step 2).

  The eight instruments:
    "sanctions"  ECONOMIC sanctions by a government or a body of
                 governments: a designation, listing, delisting, asset
                 freeze, general licence, entity/denial-list action, a
                 country-wide embargo, or secondary tariffs on buyers
                 of a named country's goods - and waiving, easing or
                 extending them. Not a sports, disciplinary or court
                 "sanction".
    "trade"      a rule about goods crossing a border, for a category
                 of goods: tariff, quota, import or export ban, export
                 control, customs rule or guidance; or a trade, supply
                 or investment agreement BETWEEN GOVERNMENTS.
    "shipping"   the movement of goods: a strait or canal closed or
                 reopened, a blockade, a ship seized, struck, mined or
                 attacked - by anyone, in war or not - a carrier
                 suspending a route, a port closed.
    "energy"     how much energy reaches a market NOW, or its price: a
                 producer cutting or raising its own output, a quota,
                 a grid or power order, an energy price or tariff a
                 government sets, a strategic-reserve release, damage
                 that stops energy flowing, a supplier changing an
                 energy supply contract. (A state embargo on another
                 country's energy is "sanctions".)
    "conflict"   force and security: military force between states;
                 an attack by a state, a state-backed group, militants
                 or terrorists on a country's territory, forces or
                 fixed infrastructure (a refinery, a grid, a data
                 centre); sabotage; a military deployment, exercise or
                 naval mission; strikes reported in a war zone. An
                 attack on a ship is "shipping".
    "subsidies"  public money or reward to build or produce: a grant,
                 subsidy, tax credit or break, a state fund, state-
                 owned investor, development bank or export-credit
                 agency (World Bank, EXIM) lending or investing, a
                 budget allocation for an industry, a law rewarding
                 local processing or hiring, a government co-owning
                 or fast-tracking a project - and removing, cutting or
                 restricting any of these. Any TAX measure on an
                 industry is "subsidies" too - a new tax, a tax rise or
                 cut, a tax credit, or taxing an incentive away - even
                 when it is still a bill: tax is a fiscal instrument,
                 not a rule on operating.
    "regulation" a rule a government applies INSIDE its own territory
                 on how an industry may build or operate: a moratorium,
                 a zoning, permit or licence decision, an AI or data
                 law, an antitrust probe or fine (it may name one
                 company - no list is involved). Also a FORMAL
                 government process on a specific measure before the
                 decision: a bill introduced, a draft rule or a
                 public-comment period, a hearing, council meeting or
                 review held by the government body on that measure, a
                 scheduled vote, a permit application under review.
    "diplomacy"  relations between states that use no other
                 instrument: talks, summits, official visits,
                 cooperation agreements or memoranda, a minister's
                 meeting with another country or an international body,
                 threats, warnings, accusations or protests between
                 governments, a UN vote or resolution. Talks ABOUT a
                 named instrument take that instrument (tariff talks ->
                 "trade"; talks on sanctions relief -> "sanctions").

  Decide in this order, and stop at the first step that answers:
  1. WHICH ACTION. Take the concrete action the HEADLINE reports. If
     the headline names an instrument (a sanction, a tariff, a
     reserve release) next to background events, take that
     instrument. If the headline reports no action, take the one
     concrete measure in the text the article hinges on.
  2. IS IT GEOPOLITICAL. An article is geopolitical when it reports or
     analyses something a government, a court, a body of governments
     (IEA, World Bank, UN, EU count) or a state-backed group did, said
     or is formally considering, or a concrete supply or movement event
     (an output change, a supply-contract change, damage stopping
     energy, an attack on a ship, a route or port closed). Then it
     always gets a channel:
     - a decided or formally proposed measure -> its instrument;
     - analysis, opinion or a government statement ABOUT a measure or
       a state's policy -> the channel of that instrument (China's
       mineral export curbs -> "trade"; a minister on reshoring
       chipmakers -> "subsidies" or "trade", whichever the text names);
     - relations between states with no other instrument ->
       "diplomacy".
     - NO government, court, body of governments or armed group acted
       (a company's plan, deal, financing, listing, results or product;
       market or stock commentary; residents, activists or a private
       lawsuit; research; a forecast): it STILL gets the channel of the
       area its subject falls in -
         energy supply, demand or prices - power, grids, oil, gas, LNG,
         coal, uranium, and data centres' power -> "energy";
         ships, ports, sea routes, freight -> "shipping";
         goods, chips, minerals or metals moving between countries, or
         their supply chains -> "trade";
         armed groups, attacks, sabotage, security -> "conflict".
       "subsidies", "regulation", "sanctions" and "diplomacy" are
       instruments only a government uses - never give them when no
       government body acted.
     null ONLY when the subject falls in none of these areas - health
     and biotech, law and courts on non-economic matters, sports,
     hotels and property, consumer software, advertising, history.
  3. WHICH INSTRUMENT. Match it to the eight above. Where two fit, the
     more specific wins, in this order:
       sanctions > trade > shipping > energy > conflict > subsidies >
       regulation > diplomacy
     A broad word never beats a named instrument: an export control is
     "trade" though it is a rule; a power order on data centres is
     "energy" though it is a rule.
     "regulation" is the LAST resort among measures. Before answering
     it, check in turn: is it about tax or public money ->
     "subsidies"; energy prices, tariffs or supply -> "energy"; goods
     crossing a border (an import or export ban, of anything) ->
     "trade"; security, sabotage or a security contract -> "conflict";
     relations between states -> "diplomacy". Only a rule on how an
     industry may build or operate at home, made or formally under
     way by a NAMED government body, is "regulation". A story merely
     ABOUT data centres, mines or pipelines - a company, residents,
     researchers, a market forecast - with no government body acting
     takes the channel of its area (step 2: data centres and pipelines
     -> "energy", mines -> "trade"), never "regulation".
  Boundaries:
  - A court ruling, a lawmakers' bill or a regulator's draft takes the
    channel of the measure concerned (a ruling on tariffs -> "trade";
    a sanctions bill introduced -> "sanctions").
  - A rule that REWARDS building is "subsidies" even when written as a
    law; one that LIMITS, pauses or refuses is "regulation".
  - Funding or fast-tracking a FUTURE energy project is "subsidies"; a
    permit or moratorium on one is "regulation"; neither is "energy",
    which is about today's flow.
  - Sabotage or a security operation that stops energy flowing is
    "energy"; one that does not is "conflict" (troops arrest pipeline
    vandals -> "conflict").
  - A contract between companies is not "trade": "trade" agreements
    are between governments.

    "Envoy says both countries discuss joint AI projects" -> diplomacy

    "Uranium miner raises $3m in a placement"   -> energy (uranium supply)

    READ THE WHOLE ARTICLE TEXT BEFORE DECIDING. The headline is
    the least informative part of the item and is often
    deliberately terse or bureaucratic ("Notice of OFAC Sanctions
    Action", "Order Renewing Temporary Denial of Export
    Privileges"). The goods, entities and sectors a measure
    actually covers are usually named in the body, not the title.
    Judging from the headline alone is the single most common way
    this field goes wrong. Where article text is provided, it -
    not the headline - is the authority.
    Decide in two steps, and keep them separate:
    (1) Does the article text (or, failing that, the headline)
        NAME a specific good, sector, industry, or a named entity
        whose own line of business is stated? A named sector is an
        explicit noun for an industry or line of business -
        "banks", "airlines", "nickel", "dairy", "shipping
        containers", "crypto exchange", "semiconductors". Naming
        only a country, a person, or a measure ("sanctions",
        "tariffs", "import bans", "export controls") with nothing
        else is NOT naming a sector. But a measure applied to a
        NAMED entity whose business the text states IS naming one -
        "denial of export privileges to an airline operating
        aircraft subject to the EAR" names aviation and export-
        controlled goods, even though the title says neither.
    (2) If a sector IS named, map it to the closest category in the
        list below whenever that category plausibly transmits -
        even when the link to AI is indirect, and even when the
        story is not about technology. Do not withhold a category
        merely because the event feels unrelated to AI; step 1 has
        already established the sector is stated.
        READ EACH CATEGORY'S FULL SCOPE LINE before deciding
        nothing fits. The list under each category name is its
        definition, not a set of illustrations: because Raw
        Materials & Critical Minerals reads "Copper, rare earths,
        lithium, aluminum, uranium feedstock...", copper mining
        and lithium mining ARE that category. If the sector the
        text names appears in a category's scope line, that
        category applies - do not then reason about whether it
        feels AI-related.
        The same holds for a recognised part of something listed.
        A scope line cannot enumerate everything: "submarine and
        subsea cables" sits under Telecom & Connectivity, lidar
        and autonomy systems under Robotics & Physical AI,
        pharmaceutical APIs under Life Sciences & Healthcare AI,
        HVAC and electrical plant for data centres under Data
        Centers & Physical Infrastructure. Ask whether the named
        sector is a kind of something in the scope line, not
        whether the exact word appears.
        Automotive deserves care, because the taxonomy covers part
        of it and not the rest. Robotics & Physical AI names "EV
        manufacturers, autonomous vehicles, ADAS", so a measure
        reaching electric vehicles, self-driving systems, lidar or
        vehicle autonomy IS that category. A measure reaching the
        conventional vehicle industry - internal-combustion
        assembly, dealerships, parts for ordinary cars - is not,
        and neither is rail. Decide on which part of the industry
        the text actually names, not on the word "automotive".
        Only when the named sector sits outside every scope line -
        aviation, rail, dairy, tourism, hospitality, conventional
        vehicle manufacturing - return an empty array rather than
        forcing a poor fit.
    If NO sector is named ANYWHERE in the text you were given,
    return an empty array - do not guess which categories a vague
    action might plausibly touch, and do not infer a sector from
    the intent or likely effect of a measure. A vaguer news item
    must never produce MORE categories than one that actually
    names what's affected.
"event_date": the date the ACTION took effect or was taken, as
  "YYYY-MM-DD", or null. This is not the date the document was
  published: a Federal Register notice published on 13 October
  can be publishing a determination issued on 1 October, or a
  general licence issued on 25 June. Read the text for the
  operative date - "issued on", "determined on", "effective",
  "signed" - and use that. Where the text gives only a
  publication date, or none, use null rather than guessing.
"event_location": WHERE the event physically happened, as the text
  names it, or null. One value, never a list.
  Prefer the most SPECIFIC place the text gives: "Strait of
  Hormuz" over "Middle East", "El Dorado County, California"
  over "United States", a named province over its country.
  The location is where the event OCCURRED or where the
  facility SITS - not where a document was signed, published
  or announced. A Federal Register notice is published in
  Washington and that is almost never the answer.
  A COUNTRY IS A VALID ANSWER when the event happened there
  and the text names nothing finer. "Fighting continues in
  Ukraine" with no city named is "Ukraine", not null - the
  country is the most specific place the text gives, which
  is the rule above. Only go finer when the text goes finer.
  NULL IS THE RIGHT ANSWER FOR A LEGAL INSTRUMENT, because
  no physical event occurred. A sanctions designation, an
  export-control rule or a tariff is an act of law: "US
  imposes Iran-related sanctions on Iraq Development Bank"
  happened in no location, so answer null. Do not reach for
  the country a measure APPLIES TO - that country is already
  recorded in "entities" as type "country", and naming it
  here would state a party as if it were a place.
  The test is whether something PHYSICALLY HAPPENED
  somewhere: a strike, an attack, a sinking, an explosion, a
  plant or data centre being built or blocked, a pipeline,
  port, canal or strait being closed or hit, fighting,
  protests, a disaster. Those get a place - a country where
  that is all the text names, something finer where it does.
  A document being issued, published or signed is not a
  physical event.
  Never name a place the text does not state.

"generated_summary": the article, compressed. Write it so a reader
  who will NOT open the source still knows everything that matters
  in it. Aim for 3 to 5 sentences; go to 6 only if the document
  genuinely carries that many distinct facts.
    Write for a reader who has to decide whether this changes
    anything they hold. That reader needs WHAT CHANGED, HOW BIG,
    WHO IS CAUGHT BY IT, and FROM WHEN - in that order, in the
    first two sentences wherever the text supports it. The order
    below is the summary's order; it is NOT the document's order,
    and you must reorder the document's facts to fit it.
      1. The operative change, in plain commercial terms - who did
         what, to whom, and whether it is in force, effective on a
         stated date, or renewed. Lead with the effect, not the
         legal mechanism: "Iran's automotive and rail sectors are
         now sanctioned - anyone operating in them is blocked from
         US jurisdiction", not "section 1(a)(i) of E.O. 13902 has
         been determined to apply to...". Name the instrument
         later in the sentence or not at all.
      2. Scope and magnitude - the named goods, sectors, companies,
         countries, codes or programmes covered, the counts and
         amounts (77 individuals, 823 firms, 25%, $500m), and any
         carve-out or exemption. If the text names components,
         minerals, chip types, vessels or ports, name them here;
         this is usually the most decision-relevant line in the
         summary, so it must not be buried below procedure.
      3. The dates that govern it - when it was issued or takes
         effect, and any expiry, review or deadline. State these
         only where the TEXT gives them. Never write the
         publication date: the row carries it already, and the
         reader can see it beside the summary.
      4. Stated cause or justification, and any stated consequence -
         but only as the text gives it, never your own inference.
         One clause is usually enough. Drop it entirely when it is
         standard recital language that would appear in any action
         under the same authority ("intended to address threats to
         national security, foreign policy and the economy").
    Name things rather than gesturing at them. "sanctions 77
    individuals involved in elections in occupied Donetsk, Luhansk
    and Crimea" is the summary; "sanctions certain individuals" is
    not. If the text names a company, a ticker, a mineral, a chip
    type, a vessel or a port, the summary names it too.
    EVERY WORD MUST COME FROM THE PROVIDED TEXT. This is the one
    rule with no exceptions:
      - Never state a number, date, percentage, amount or duration
        that does not appear in the text. Not an estimate, not a
        rounding, not a figure you know from similar measures.
      - Never name a company, country, sector or person the text
        does not name.
      - Never assert an effect, cost, market consequence or
        transmission path unless the text itself asserts it. If it
        does not say a measure raises input costs, the summary does
        not say so either.
      - Never fill a gap from general knowledge. An unstated fact
        is simply absent from the summary.
      A fabricated specific reads as authoritative and nothing
      downstream can catch it, so when in doubt leave it out.
    Omit: restatements of the headline, that a document was
    published or posted to a website, docket and CFR numbers,
    "for further information contact", legal authority recitals,
    and any sentence that would be equally true of a different
    measure. If deleting a sentence loses no fact, delete it.
    Apply that test strictly - these all failed it on real rows:
      - "The action is intended to address threats to U.S.
        national security, foreign policy, and economy as
        described in E.O. 13902" - recited in every action under
        that authority, so it distinguishes nothing.
      - "U.S. financial institutions may rely on the originator
        of a funds transfer for compliance" - standard
        due-diligence wording, not a fact about this measure.
      - "The license does not unblock any property or authorize
        activities prohibited by other regulations" - the default
        for every general licence.
      - "The EU condemned the elections and stated it would never
        recognise them" - a stated position, not a change.
    A sentence of rhetoric or standard recital crowds out a
    sentence of scope. When the text supports both, the scope
    sentence wins.
    Write plainly, in full sentences, no bullet points, no preamble
    such as "This article reports that". Start with the action.
    If the text supports fewer than 3 sentences of real content,
    write fewer. If it supports none, use null - never pad.

If the available text lacks information for a field, use "unclear" or
